Abstract 

Classical theories of cognition describe problem solving as exploratory search through struc-tured problem spaces in which repeated interaction gradually compresses search into efficient representational structures. Predictive artificial intelligence systems introduce a distinct regime in which stabilization may occur before exploratory diversification unfolds, supplying solutions and decision trajectories prior to internally generated search. This paper develops a geomet-ric dynamical framework in which attention evolves over a landscape of strategies shaped by stabilizing drift, endogenous exploratory perturbation, and responsiveness-gated learning. Pre-dictive assistance is modeled as a process of exogenous exploratory compression that stabilizes trajectories before self-generated exploration broadens the accessible regions of strategy space. The framework yields three main results. First, sustained predictive stabilization reduces ex-ploratory responsiveness by attenuating the effective influence of intrinsic perturbations even when exploratory variability remains present. Second, curvature accumulates and relaxes asym-metrically, producing hysteresis and delayed recovery of exploratory mobility after assistance withdrawal. Third, developmental outcomes depend critically on the timing of stabilization, with early intervention narrowing future exploratory traversal before broad representational di-versification has occurred. The framework generates empirically testable predictions concerning exploratory entropy, premature convergence, and delayed recovery following predictive stabi-lization. More broadly, the results suggest that predictive systems may reshape the geometry of exploratory cognition itself. 

# 1 Introduction 

Classical cognitive science describes problem solving as exploratory search through structured prob-lem spaces rather than exhaustive optimization [1, 2, 3]. Expertise emerges through repeated ex-ploratory engagement that gradually compresses search into efficient representational structures and stable solution routines [4, 1, 5]. In this classical picture, compression follows exploratory diversification. Predictive artificial intelligence systems introduce a different regime. Increasingly, such systems intervene before uncertainty is fully experienced, supplying solutions, completions, and candidate trajectories prior to internally generated search [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. These systems improve short-run performance, but their long-run effects on exploratory cognition remain poorly understood. The central concern of the present work is therefore not cognitive replacement but altered compression dynamics. Predictive systems may stabilize trajectories before exploratory diversification unfolds, narrowing the range of sampled alternatives and progressively compressing exploratory mobility across cognitive landscapes. 1

Existing AI safety and social-impact frameworks have focused primarily on misinformation, fair-ness, robustness, and task-level forms of automation overreliance [11, 12]. More recently, emerging work has begun to examine how agentic AI systems may erode learning incentives and contribute to long-run degradation of collective knowledge formation [13]. Parallel findings across GPS nav-igation, automation, cognitive offloading, and AI-assisted expertise likewise suggest that predic-tive systems can alter exploratory engagement, cognitive mapping, and skill acquisition over time [14, 8, 15]. However, these concerns have largely remained fragmented across cognitive science, human-computer interaction, and automation research and lack a unified dynamical account of how predictive stabilization reshapes exploratory search over developmental time. A substantial literature demonstrates that difficulty, delay, and partial failure play construc-tive roles in learning [16, 17]. Exploratory engagement with uncertainty supports representational revision, hypothesis generation, and strategy discovery [18, 19]. Such exploration is often sus-tained by endogenous fluctuations including curiosity, spontaneous thought, and prediction error [20, 21, 22, 23]. Importantly, the present framework does not imply that all forms of predictive assis-tance necessarily suppress exploration. Predictive systems may also preserve or amplify exploratory branching by encouraging divergent reasoning or delaying convergence. The critical distinction is therefore not between assistance and non-assistance, but between stabilizing interventions that pre-maturely compress exploratory search and exploratory interventions that preserve broad traversal across alternative representations. To analyze this process, we develop a geometric dynamical framework in which attention evolves over a landscape of strategies under the joint influence of stabilizing drift and endogenous ex-ploratory perturbation [24, 25]. The geometry of this landscape governs exploratory responsiveness and adaptive mobility across representational trajectories. Shallow regions permit broad traversal across competing representations, whereas steep regions concentrate attention near locally rein-forced configurations [26, 27, 28]. Predictive assistance enters as a curvature-modifying process that stabilizes trajectories before exploratory diversification has sufficiently expanded the acces-sible regions of strategy space. Compression therefore occurs exogenously rather than emerging gradually through exploratory traversal itself. The central implication is that adaptive rigidity depends not only on biological plasticity, but also on accumulated stabilization history embedded within the geometry of exploratory search [29, 30, 31]. Early predictive stabilization can narrow future exploratory mobility before broad representational repertoires have formed [32, 17, 33]. Because stabilization accumulates and re-laxes asymmetrically, its effects may persist after assistance is withdrawn, producing hysteresis, metastable trapping, and path dependence in exploratory behavior [34, 35]. The framework developed below formalizes these processes through a minimal dynamical model linking exploratory attention, responsiveness, and curvature dynamics. The model generates struc-tural consequences including hysteresis, premature convergence, nonlinear escape dynamics, and timing-sensitive rigidity under predictive stabilization. It also generates empirically testable pre-dictions concerning exploratory entropy, delayed recovery, and long-run compression of exploratory mobility. 

# 2 A Geometric Perspective 

We formalize deliberation as motion over a structured landscape of strategies in which attention evolves under the joint influence of stabilizing drift and endogenous exploratory variation, consis-tent with dynamical systems and attractor-based accounts of cognition [24, 28]. The landscape encodes relative effort, stability, and traversal difficulty across cognitive configurations. Shallow 2regions permit broad exploratory traversal, whereas steep regions concentrate attention within narrow and locally reinforced trajectories. In this framework, exploratory search corresponds to movement across regions of varying curvature, and stabilized expertise corresponds to concentration of trajectories into efficient basins of attraction. Exploration is treated as a constitutive feature of adaptive cognition rather than an external disturbance. Endogenous fluctuations arising from uncertainty, curiosity, associative drift, and prediction error continually displace attention from locally stable states, enabling transitions across alternative representational configurations. Plasticity determines whether such perturbations can effectively redirect trajectories and reshape internal representations. As basins deepen, exploratory variability may remain present, yet its capacity to generate successful traversal across competing regions of strategy space progressively declines. Predictive assistance introduces a distinct form of exogenous stabilization. By supplying ex-ternally stabilized trajectories before exploratory diversification unfolds, predictive systems may compress the space of explored alternatives prior to broad representational sampling. Within the present framework, predictive assistance therefore acts as a curvature-modifying process that reshapes the geometry of exploratory search itself. The model developed below formalizes this interaction through coupled dynamics linking exploratory attention, responsiveness, and curvature accumulation.

## 3.1 Assistance as Geometric Deformation 

Within the present framework, predictive assistance acts as a form of exogenous compression that reshapes the geometry of cognitive search itself. In classical expertise formation, stabilization emerges gradually through repeated exploratory engagement and self-generated representational restructuring. Predictive systems introduce a different regime in which stabilization may occur be-fore exploratory diversification has sufficiently broadened the accessible regions of strategy space. By supplying externally stabilized trajectories in advance of uncertainty-driven exploration, pre-dictive assistance compresses exploratory mobility prior to the formation of flexible internal repre-sentations. This process alters the geometry through which deliberation unfolds. Anticipatory assistance and adaptive difficulty regulation repeatedly stabilize particular regions of the landscape by re-solving uncertainty before exploratory traversal develops. Over time, these interventions deepen local basins of attraction, increase local curvature, and reduce the effective influence of intrinsic 4perturbations. Curvature therefore functions not merely as a geometric property of the landscape, but as an accumulated structural record of prior stabilization history and exploratory compression. We model curvature as evolving according to 

∂tκ(x, t ) = α1A1(x, t ) + α2A2(x, t ) − α↓κ(x, t ),

where A1 and A2 represent two distinct mechanisms through which predictive systems compress exploratory search. The term A1 represents anticipatory assistance in which externally supplied structure stabilizes trajectories before self-generated exploration unfolds. Examples include predictive text completion, AI-generated outlines, suggested solution paths, automated code completion, or decision-support systems that provide candidate representations before uncertainty-driven search has expanded the space of alternatives [8, 15]. In geometric terms, A1 compresses exploratory search by direct-ing attention toward pre-stabilized regions of strategy space and reducing exploratory branching early in deliberation. Such systems may improve local efficiency and reduce uncertainty during problem solving while simultaneously reducing the amount of internally generated decomposition, representational revision, and exploratory traversal required for adaptive restructuring. Systems may therefore become increasingly efficient within familiar trajectories while progressively losing exploratory mobility across unfamiliar conceptual regions. The term A2 represents adaptive difficulty regulation in which predictive systems reduce ex-ploratory demand by simplifying the search environment itself. Examples include automated hint systems, dynamic task simplification, adaptive tutoring systems that remove difficult intermediate steps, or turn-by-turn GPS navigation systems that reduce the need for self-guided spatial explo-ration [14]. Unlike A1, which stabilizes specific trajectories directly, A2 compresses exploratory search indirectly by reducing the uncertainty gradients that would otherwise generate broad ex-ploratory variation and sustained traversal across competing representational regions. In geometric terms, A2 smooths the landscape by lowering the effective traversal costs associated with uncer-tainty navigation and exploratory restructuring. Consistent with this interpretation, externally guided navigation systems have been associated with reduced landmark encoding, diminished cog-nitive mapping ability, and greater reliance on rigid stimulus-response strategies during navigation [14]. In contrast, prolonged self-guided navigation and active cognitive map construction, as ob-served in London taxi drivers, appear to preserve broad exploratory engagement, flexible traversal, and adaptive spatial representations [36]. The final term, α↓κ(x, t ), represents gradual relaxation of compressed structure when predictive stabilization ceases and endogenous exploratory variability re-engages the landscape. In the ab-sence of continued reinforcement, exploratory perturbations, representational competition, and en-vironmental variability gradually reduce geometric steepness and permit re-expansion of accessible search trajectories. The present formulation models this relaxation as a continuous decay process, but effective relaxation capacity may itself depend on developmental timing and accumulated sta-bilization history. During sensitive developmental intervals, repeated predictive stabilization may produce forms of exploratory compression that become increasingly resistant to subsequent re-expansion. In such regimes, effective relaxation may proceed extremely slowly, causing compressed exploratory structures to persist long after the original stabilizing conditions have disappeared. The geometry of exploratory search may therefore age independently of the organism itself, reflecting accumulated stabilization history rather than biological age alone. Because accumulation and relaxation operate on different time scales, the geometry of atten-tion becomes path dependent. Repeated anticipatory stabilization and difficulty regulation can therefore produce persistent exploratory compression even after assistance is withdrawn. Stabi-lized trajectories continue to channel subsequent search into narrow and locally reinforced regions 5of strategy space, producing hysteresis, metastable trapping, and delayed recovery of exploratory mobility. Predictive assistance consequently reshapes not only momentary deliberation, but also the long-run geometry through which future exploration, revision, and adaptive traversal remain possible.

## 6.1 Principle 1: Curvature Monitoring 

Because long-run rigidity is governed by curvature accumulation, the primary control variable in educational and predictive AI systems is geometric steepness rather than immediate performance. The curvature dynamics imply that repeated predictive stabilization increases κ(x, t ) whenever stabilization pressure exceeds the rate at which compressed exploratory structure relaxes. In practical terms, assistance intensity cannot be evaluated solely through task success, local pro-ductivity, or short-run efficiency gains, since stabilization may continue compressing exploratory mobility even while immediate performance appears to improve. The relevant question is therefore whether predictive support is producing cumulative exploratory compression faster than endoge-nous exploratory processes can restore adaptive flexibility and representational traversal across the landscape. Although curvature is not directly observable, behavioral proxies can approximate its accu-mulation through interaction traces and patterns of exploratory behavior. Within the present framework, increasing geometric steepness corresponds behaviorally to progressive concentration of exploratory search into a narrower subset of repeatedly stabilized trajectories. As basins deepen, at-tention becomes increasingly likely to revisit previously reinforced representations and increasingly unlikely to traverse alternative conceptual regions or generate divergent representational pathways. This compression manifests behaviorally through increasing reliance on predictive completions, re-duced spontaneous strategy variation, diminished self-initiated revision, lower exploratory entropy, decreased branching across alternative representations, and reduced responsiveness to corrective feedback. At a broader cognitive level, this interpretation is consistent with experience-dependent stabilization processes associated with representational entrenchment, habitization, and declining exploratory responsiveness following repeated reinforcement. Monitoring such indicators therefore permits indirect estimation of whether stabilization is progressively narrowing exploratory mobility and constraining future adaptive traversal across the landscape. The necessity of monitoring follows directly from the nonlinear character of the escape dynam-ics. Because escape times scale exponentially with curvature, exploratory mobility may appear behaviorally stable across extended intervals before collapsing abruptly once curvature crosses a critical regime. Compressed trajectories then continue to channel subsequent search into narrow and locally reinforced regions of strategy space, making spontaneous recovery increasingly difficult. Predictive systems optimized purely around immediate convergence may therefore permit substan-tial exploratory compression to accumulate while the resulting rigidity remains behaviorally latent. 10 Time

## 6.2 Principle 2: Pulsed Assistance 

The curvature dynamics imply that continuous predictive stabilization risks cumulative exploratory compression whenever accumulation persistently outpaces relaxation. Because curvature increases under predictive stabilization and relaxes only gradually through endogenous exploratory processes, sustained assistance without interruption can produce monotonic growth in geometric steepness, particularly when relaxation rates are modest. Under such conditions, the system may no longer possess sufficiently strong exploratory responsiveness to counterbalance accumulated stabilization. Continuous optimization can therefore progressively compress exploratory mobility even while im-mediate task performance continues to improve. Pulsed assistance follows directly from this asymmetry between stabilization and relaxation. By intermittently withholding predictive intervention, the system permits curvature to dissipate between stabilization episodes and allows endogenous perturbations to regain effective influence 11 over exploratory traversal. During these intervals, attention can redistribute across competing representational regions rather than remaining confined within previously stabilized trajectories. The objective is therefore not to eliminate assistance, but to regulate its temporal profile such that average curvature accumulation remains bounded relative to exploratory relaxation across relevant developmental intervals. ## 6.3 Principle 3: Early-Phase Protection 

The integral form of the curvature dynamics implies that early stabilization events carry dispropor-tionate structural weight. Because curvature accumulates cumulatively and relaxes only gradually, assistance introduced during formative exploratory phases can reshape baseline geometry before alternative regions of the landscape have been sufficiently sampled. When predictive support stabi-lizes specific strategies prior to exploratory diversification, it effectively pre-structures subsequent deliberative trajectories. Later perturbations therefore operate within a geometry that has already narrowed and become locally reinforced through early stabilization history. Early-phase protection follows directly from this path dependence. During initial learning intervals, endogenous exploration plays a disproportionate role in distributing attention across competing representational regions and expanding the accessible topology of strategy space. Heavy predictive intervention during this stage accelerates curvature accumulation before exploratory diversification and relaxation processes can counterbalance it. The result is not merely temporary reliance on external support, but long-run asymmetry in the geometry of exploratory search itself. Early stabilization compresses exploratory mobility before broad representational repertoires have formed. Consider a child first learning arithmetic. Under unconstrained exploration, the learner may count on fingers, regroup quantities, approximate intermediate values, or test multiple decomposi-tions before convergence. These exploratory detours distribute attention across multiple representa-tional pathways and broaden the geometry of subsequent search. Exploratory variability therefore functions as a mechanism for expanding future adaptive mobility across the landscape. If predictive assistance immediately stabilizes a preferred strategy, however, the distribution of sampled alter-natives narrows before flexible exploratory structure has formed. The learner may achieve rapid task success while simultaneously developing a more compressed exploratory topology organized around a narrower subset of stabilized trajectories. Because accumulated structural history can dominate biological responsiveness, limiting assis-tance during early exploration is not merely a developmental preference but a geometric safeguard against premature exploratory compression. Assistance introduced after exploratory diversification has already occurred operates on a broader and more distributed landscape and therefore produces less persistent deformation. In contrast, sustained predictive stabilization during formative phases may generate elevated baseline curvature that persists even when relaxation rates remain high. Systems may consequently become locally efficient within familiar representational routines while exhibiting reduced exploratory mobility across unfamiliar conceptual trajectories. This implica-tion contrasts sharply with educational systems optimized primarily around rapid convergence, frictionless performance, and immediate task completion. Within the present framework, such optimization may improve short-run efficiency while simultaneously compressing the exploratory diversification required for long-run adaptive flexibility. Operationally, early-phase protection requires adaptive modulation of assistance based on de-velopmental stage and exploratory structure rather than task difficulty alone. Systems should privilege self-generated exploration during initial exposure to unfamiliar domains and progressively 13 introduce predictive support only after sufficiently broad exploratory branching has occurred. In practical terms, this may involve delaying predictive completion, restricting automated hints dur-ing early learning phases, requiring generative reconstruction before assistance becomes available, or gating AI support until multiple candidate representations have been explored. The governing logic remains geometric. Prevent curvature accumulation from outpacing landscape expansion dur-ing structurally sensitive intervals in which exploratory diversification is constructing the future topology of adaptive search. 

## 6.4 Principle 4: Decay Restoration 

Hysteresis implies that removing predictive assistance alone may be insufficient to restore ex-ploratory flexibility once curvature has accumulated. Because curvature relaxes only gradually at rate α↓(a), recovery may proceed slowly even after stabilization pressure ceases. When predic-tive stabilization dominates relaxation over sustained intervals, the resulting geometric steepness can persist long beyond the original intervention, leaving exploratory search constrained by previ-ously compressed trajectories and locally reinforced representational routines. Decay restoration addresses this structural lag by treating exploratory recovery as an active geometric process rather than a passive consequence of assistance withdrawal. Within the present framework, restoration corresponds to increasing the effective influence of ex-ploratory perturbations relative to accumulated curvature and thereby accelerating the re-expansion of compressed regions of strategy space. Rather than merely withholding support, educational and predictive systems may actively amplify processes that counteract stabilization and redistribute attention across alternative trajectories. Activities that force strategy variation, introduce deliber-ate ambiguity, require generative reconstruction of solutions, delay externally supplied structure, or sustain exploratory branching function as geometric flattening operations that weaken locally reinforced basins and restore exploratory mobility across the landscape. The necessity of restoration follows directly from the nonlinear escape dynamics of the system. Because escape times scale exponentially with curvature, spontaneous recovery of exploratory re-sponsiveness becomes increasingly improbable once geometric steepness surpasses moderate levels. Passive waiting for relaxation may therefore be insufficient to restore broad exploratory traversal within practical developmental timescales. Structured interventions that temporarily increase ex-ploratory pressure, suspend predictive stabilization, require adversarial reasoning, or force traver-sal across competing representational pathways can shift the balance toward relaxation and ex-ploratory re-diversification. In this framework, exploratory flexibility is not treated as a naturally self-correcting property, but as a maintained dynamical equilibrium whose persistence depends on preserving sufficient exploratory breadth within the evolving geometry of cognition. The objective of restoration is therefore not merely to halt further stabilization, but to reduce accumulated curvature to levels at which endogenous perturbations regain effective leverage over future exploratory trajectories. Recovery requires more than removing predictive compression. It requires partial reconstruction of the exploratory mobility through which adaptive traversal across unfamiliar conceptual regions remains possible. The framework does not imply that exploratory compression is universally undesirable. In time-critical or safety-critical environments, rapid convergence may be the intended objective. Aviation protocols, emergency medical triage, military coordination systems, and standardized operational procedures often require suppression of broad exploratory branching in favor of reliable convergence onto a narrow set of validated actions. In such domains, predictive stabilization may be beneficial because it deepens task-relevant basins, reduces uncertainty, and minimizes costly exploratory wandering under high-risk conditions. The developmental concern arises when similar stabilization 14 regimes are applied during phases in which broad exploratory traversal is required for the formation of flexible internal representations and adaptive responsiveness. The framework is therefore normatively neutral with respect to stabilization itself. The same ge-ometric processes that generate maladaptive rigidity in open-ended developmental learning may be desirable in high-reliability environments where rapid convergence and suppression of exploratory variation are operational requirements. The central question is therefore not whether exploratory compression occurs, but whether the surrounding task domain benefits from preserving or mini-mizing exploratory mobility across the landscape. 

# 7 Empirical Signatures of Exploratory Compression 

The framework generates empirical predictions concerning how predictive assistance reshapes ex-ploratory search. The central prediction is that predictive systems can compress exploratory mobil-ity even when immediate task performance remains stable or improves. A learner using predictive assistance may produce faster or more polished outputs while simultaneously traversing a narrower range of representational trajectories during learning itself. Consider a student drafting an analytical essay. Under unconstrained exploration, the student may generate multiple outlines, reorganize conceptual structure, and revise competing interpreta-tions before convergence. These revisions and detours reflect broad exploratory traversal across the cognitive landscape. Under strong predictive completion, however, a coherent outline may appear before exploratory branching has sufficiently diversified the accessible regions of strategy space. The resulting essay may improve immediately in fluency or organization, yet the distribution of explored alternatives becomes narrower and increasingly concentrated around externally stabilized trajectories. The framework predicts that such compression should become behaviorally observable through reduced exploratory entropy, diminished branching across alternative strategies, accelerated con-vergence onto stabilized representations, reduced representational displacement during revision, and slower recovery of exploratory mobility following assistance withdrawal. The critical empiri-cal implication is therefore not that predictive systems necessarily reduce output quality, but that they may alter the geometry through which exploratory traversal, conceptual restructuring, and adaptive revision occur during learning itself. More broadly, exploratory compression should appear most strongly in domains requiring broad representational traversal, uncertainty navigation, and self-generated restructuring prior to conver-gence. Open-ended reasoning, scientific hypothesis generation, conceptual explanation, mathe-matical decomposition, navigation, design, and creative problem solving should therefore exhibit stronger compression effects than domains dominated by routine execution or standardized pro-cedural recall. Because curvature accumulation operates cumulatively over developmental time, repeated stabilization may generate delayed and path-dependent reductions in exploratory respon-siveness even when short-run performance remains positive throughout the compression process.

A central prediction of the framework is that the timing of predictive stabilization matters in-dependently of the total quantity of assistance provided. The framework predicts that early stabilization should suppress the normal expansion of ex-ploratory entropy by reducing branching across alternative strategies, accelerating convergence onto externally stabilized trajectories, and decreasing the diversity of self-generated representations even when immediate task performance remains stable or improves. As curvature accumulates, ex-ploratory search becomes increasingly concentrated within narrow and locally reinforced regions of strategy space, producing lower exploratory entropy, reduced revision depth, and diminished exploratory mobility during subsequent unaided problem solving. Premature convergence therefore reflects more than rapid solution acquisition. It reflects com-pression of exploratory traversal before broad representational diversification has occurred. Because curvature accumulation is path dependent, early stabilization can continue shaping exploratory ge-ometry long after predictive support itself has been withdrawn. 

## 7.3 Recovery and Hysteresis 

The strongest empirical signature of the framework concerns recovery following assistance with-drawal. If predictive stabilization produces hysteresis, exploratory responsiveness should not im-mediately recover once support is removed. Instead, compressed exploratory structure should persist and continue constraining subsequent traversal even after stabilization has ceased. Future search therefore proceeds from a landscape already reshaped by accumulated stabilization history. This prediction may be evaluated by removing assistance following repeated exposure and mea-suring recovery across subsequent tasks. Relevant behavioral measures include re-expansion of exploratory entropy, latency to generate alternative strategies, revision depth following contradic-tion, branching across newly generated solution paths, and escape time from initially stabilized trajectories. The framework predicts that participants exposed to sustained early stabilization should exhibit slower recovery of exploratory mobility than participants exposed to delayed or temporally distributed assistance, even after predictive support has been withdrawn. In behavioral terms, hysteresis appears as persistent narrowing of exploratory search, reduced flexibility in revising stabilized representations, delayed re-expansion of exploratory branching, and continued concentration of deliberation within previously reinforced trajectories following prior compression. Exploratory variability may remain behaviorally present, yet its capacity to redirect attention remains reduced because accumulated curvature continues constraining traversal across the landscape. # 8 Conclusion 

Classical theories of cognition describe problem solving as exploratory search through structured problem spaces in which stabilization and compression emerge gradually through exploratory en-gagement itself [5, 2, 4]. Predictive artificial intelligence systems introduce a different regime in which stabilization may occur before exploratory diversification unfolds. The present framework develops a geometric account of how predictive assistance reshapes exploratory cognition through curvature accumulation, hysteresis, and path-dependent compression of exploratory search. The model formalizes deliberation as exploratory motion over a landscape of strategies shaped by stabilizing drift, endogenous perturbation, and responsiveness-gated learning. Predictive as-sistance acts as a curvature-modifying process that can compress exploratory trajectories before internally generated search has sufficiently diversified. As curvature accumulates, exploratory re-sponsiveness declines and future traversal across unfamiliar conceptual regions becomes increasingly constrained. Because curvature relaxes only gradually, compressed exploratory structures may per-sist even after assistance is withdrawn, producing hysteresis, delayed recovery, and metastable rigidity. The framework further suggests that the long-run effects of predictive assistance are structural and developmental rather than merely task-specific. Early stabilization can narrow the accessible regions of strategy space before broad representational repertoires emerge, producing persistent reductions in exploratory mobility even when short-run performance improves. The model con-sequently generates empirical predictions concerning exploratory entropy, premature convergence, and delayed recovery following assistance withdrawal. More broadly, the framework suggests that predictive systems should be evaluated not only by how efficiently they guide behavior, but also by how they reshape the long-run geometry of exploratory cognition itself. The central contribution of this work is therefore to formalize a new class of geometric and developmental dynamics through which predictive assistance may alter the conditions under which exploratory traversal, representational revision, and adaptive flexibility remain possible. 19 References
